Binder spine
insert
The card that faces out of the binder holding the twelve folio pages. It is the page masthead turned through 90° - the same cream band, the same 38 pt wordmark, the same slate rule and the same 60° chapter transition - so the object on the shelf and the pages inside it are recognisably one piece of work. The area block carries all four chapter fields in order, because the spine stands for the whole folio rather than for one section of it: four bands of equal length, each joined to the next by that same 60° edge, closing in cream so the strip begins and ends in the masthead's own colour.

Specification

Trim size59 × 436 mm (5.9 × 43.6 cm)

SheetA2 portrait, 420 × 594 mm, two copies
Stock200-250 gsm matt, uncoated
ColourCMYK, 300 dpi

Printing

  1. Export this sheet to PDF at A2, CMYK, 300 dpi.
  2. Print at 100 per cent. Do not fit to page - any scaling changes the trim and the strip will no longer seat in the pocket.
  3. Check one measured dimension on the proof before trimming: the cream band should read 44 mm across the finished strip.

Finishing

  1. Trim to the crop marks, cutting just inside the line so no white edge is left on the cream.
  2. Test-fit in the spine pocket. If the binder is a little narrower, trim the excess from the foot end, never the head - the wordmark and its slate rule are set from the head.
  3. Slide in with the wordmark at the top. The second copy is a spare; keep it flat.
Check before printing. The trim is taken from the sizes given for the binder, so measure the spine pocket itself first - pocket width and cover height, not the outside of the binder. 436 mm is 16 mm longer than an A3 sheet, which is why this one item cannot print on the folio's own stock.
