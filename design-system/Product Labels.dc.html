One layout, three labels
  • Every label runs the same six lines: wordmark, piece and size, fibre content, the five care symbols, a care note, and the country of origin. Only the words and the symbols change.
  • The five symbols are in the order the care standard uses: wash, bleach, tumble dry, natural dry, iron. A crossed symbol means do not.
  • The note carries only what a symbol cannot say: separately, collar off, satin reverse only.

The label on its patch

The allowance is blank on every route. The cell on sheets 1 and 2 is the patch, and only the ruled box inside it carries ink. No cut line, fold line or stitch line is printed in the allowance, so nothing shows through the fold on a fabric sheet and nothing transfers from film that should not.
Symbols first, words for the rest. The five symbols carry wash, bleach, tumble dry, natural dry and iron, in that order. The note under them carries only what a symbol cannot say. They are drawn on a 24 unit grid at a 1.4 stroke and hold at 5.5 mm; check them on the plain-paper proof before printing on stock.

Two ways onto the fabric

The same eight labels are printed twice. Sheet 1 is the artwork as it reads, for the routes where the print itself becomes the label. Sheet 2 is the artwork reversed, for the route where the print is pressed face down onto a patch. Print one sheet or the other, never both.

As it reads - sheet 1

  1. Printable fabric sheet. Print on an A4 printable cotton or polyester sheet. Peel the backing and cut on the crop marks. The sheet is the patch; there is nothing to transfer.
  2. Label printer. Send sheet 1 as a PDF and ask for 71 × 48 mm finished on white polyester or satin, artwork placed as shown, edges sealed. Any reversing is their job.

Mirrored - sheet 2

  1. Iron-on transfer. Print on transfer paper for light fabrics; it is pressed face down, so the reversed artwork reads correctly on the patch. Cut 1.5 mm outside the ruled box. Press onto a 71 × 48 mm patch of white polyester with a dry iron, no steam, synthetic setting, on a hard flat surface, from the lowest heat that bonds. Peel to the paper's instruction.

Before either sheet goes on stock

  1. Print at 100 per cent, never fit to page; the patches are at true size.
  2. Proof once on plain paper and measure the ruled box: 55 mm across. Read sheet 3 for the copy and the symbols.
  3. Never mirror in the printer driver. Each sheet carries one orientation and states it.

Stitching in

  1. Seal the patch edges: a lighter for polyester, a narrow zigzag for cotton.
  2. Position the dress label on the left side seam below the waist, the overskirt label inside the band at the left opening, and the collar label inside the stand at centre back, hidden when the collar is worn.
  3. Turn the 6 mm allowance under on the top, bottom and right edges; press lightly from the reverse, never over the print.
  4. Catch the 10 mm left allowance in the seam and stitch the other three folded edges down by hand, close to the fold.
