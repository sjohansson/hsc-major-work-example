The transfer on its patch

The allowance is cloth, not print. Iron-on film carries everything it is cut to, so no cut line, fold line or allowance is printed inside the trim. Cut the transfer to the crop marks and cut the polyester patch larger; the difference is what the seam catches.

Printing

  1. Print sheet 1 at 100 per cent on A4 transfer material. Do not fit to page — the labels are set at true size.
  2. Use transfer paper for light fabrics: the label patch is white polyester, and dark-fabric paper lays down an opaque white film that would show as a patch.
  3. Mirror the artwork with the prop, not with the printer driver, so the banner on the sheet always states how that sheet was printed. Do not mirror twice.
  4. Read the copy on sheet 2 before committing transfer paper — the mirrored sheet cannot be proofread. Then proof sheet 1 once on plain paper and check one measured dimension: the ruled box should read 55 mm across.

Applying

  1. Cut each transfer to its crop marks, square and clean. Round nothing; the ruled box is 1.5 mm inside the cut.
  2. Cut a 71 × 48 mm patch of the white polyester for each label. Seal all four raw edges with a lighter, as every other raw edge in this project.
  3. Press the patch flat and dry first. Any moisture left in it lifts the film at the edges.
  4. Transfer with a dry iron, no steam, on the synthetic setting, with the patch on a hard flat surface rather than an ironing board. Use the pressing cloth the transfer paper supplies or its backing sheet.
  5. Work up from the lowest heat that bonds. Polyester scorches, and a scorched patch is discarded, not rescued.
  6. Peel to the paper’s own instruction, hot or cold. Let the label cool fully flat before handling it.

Stitching in

  1. Position the dress label on the left side seam below the waist. Position the second dress label inside the overskirt band at the left opening. Position the collar label inside the stand at centre back, where it is hidden when the collar is worn.
  2. Turn the 6 mm allowance under on the top, bottom and right edges; press lightly from the reverse, never over the transfer.
  3. Catch the 10 mm left allowance in the side seam and stitch the remaining three folded edges down by hand, close to the fold.
One wording everywhere. This copy is page 7’s product-label text, and the swing tag repeats it. Dress and collar care lines differ because the fibres differ. Change the size, the fibre or the care in one place and it must change in all, or the folio contradicts the garment.
Words here, symbols on the tag. AS/NZS 1957 accepts written instructions or symbols. Inside a 55 × 36 mm box the ISO symbols would sit about 4 mm tall, and their interior detail fills in on transfer film at that size. The labels carry the words, the tag carries the symbols. Page 7 states this.
