## Detailed section assessment

### 1. Design Inspiration (Pages 1 to 2) - Score: 5 / 5

- Focus area relationship: The nominated focus area is costume. Page 1 explicitly explains how costume communicates character before dialogue, establishing Wednesday Addams at a school dance. The analysis details stage performance needs: distance legibility at ten metres under gym lighting, silhouette clarity, and movement during dance.
- Creative and innovative ideas: The core innovation is that the costume converts between two scenes in a median 48 seconds (Experiment 8). A detachable tiered overskirt on a separate grosgrain and petersham hooked band combines with a detachable white cotton-piqué collar on four snaps. Overskirt on and collar off provides the dance dress; overskirt off and collar on provides the school uniform dress. Tiers are graded 18, 26, and 34 cm to produce delayed movement when turning.
- Historical, cultural, and contemporary analysis:
  - Contemporary/Screen source: Netflix *Wednesday* series dance dress (Image 1) and school uniform collar (Image 7); 1964 *The Addams Family* television series (Image 6) for black-and-white tonal discipline.
  - Historical factors: British mourning dress c. 1905 (Image 2) for closed silhouette, high collar, and layered black textures; French silk evening dress c. 1926 (Image 3) for straight-grain tiers and movement; American nylon-tulle party dress c. 1955 (Image 4) for fitted bodice and tea-length volume. Explains how black serves different roles across periods without copying historical garments.
  - Technique factors: Victorian mourning jet beading c. 1890 (Image 5) for black-on-black surface reflection; 1950 horsehair braid (Image 8) for hem stand-off.
- Graphical communication: Images 1 to 8 are cleanly framed with captions explaining what each plate shows, contextual references with Met accession numbers, and screen source citations.

### 2. Visual Design Development (Pages 3 to 5) - Score: 5 / 5

- Quality of drawings and inspiration link:
  - Design One (page 3): Sketches 1A (front), 1B (back with zip), and 1C (yoke/collar) link directly to Image 1.
  - Design Two (page 3): Sketches 2A (front), 2B (back with lapped zip/hook), and 2C (bodice jet beading) link to Image 2 and Image 5.
  - Design Three (page 4): Sketches 3A (front), 3B (back), and 3C (sleeve/wrist detail) synthesize One and Two, linking to Images 3, 4, and 7.
  - Final Design (page 5): Views 4A (front on form), 4B (back on form with invisible zip and band), and 4C (convertible school dress configuration with collar and cuff).
- Functional and aesthetic analysis: Every design includes a feature table classifying items as A (aesthetic), F (functional), or A + F. Comprehensive Plus, Minus, Interesting (PMI) tables evaluate each design using formal elements and principles vocabulary (line, rhythm, texture, hue, unity, tone, proportion, emphasis). Weaknesses are critically identified (e.g. Design Two cannot be danced in; Design Three equal tiers appear static; lapped zip causes a visible ridge).
- Sequential development: Concepts progress logically from a direct copy (Design One), to an opposing historical silhouette (Design Two), to a synthesis (Design Three), and finally to the convertible solution (Final Design).

### 3. Manufacturing Specification (Pages 6 to 8) - Score: 5 / 5

- Description: Page 6 details the size 10 two-scene costume, finished body measurements (bust 88, waist 68, hip 96, shoulder width 36, shoulder to waist 44, waist to hem 68, sleeve 54/58, cuff 22, neck 38), commercial pattern source (Harrow and Vane 2140, view B, size 10), and itemised pattern modifications with rationale.
- Production drawings:
  - Drawings 6.1 (Front) and 6.2 (Back) are drawn to 1:10 scale. A ruler check confirms the drawn dress height is 112.1 mm, matching the 112 cm finished length to within 0.1 mm. Grainlines, facing outlines, seam allowances, and dimensions are fully specified.
  - Detail drawings include 6.3 Collar and stand (1:5 stand, 1:10 collar), 6.4 Flared overskirt (1:20 scale, 32 mm drawn depth for 64 cm finished carrier), 6.5 Band overlap (1:5 scale, 30 mm drawn width for 15 cm overlap), and 6.6 Cuff section (1:2 scale).
  - Page 7 provides full pattern pieces drawn to 1:15 scale with wine-coloured alteration outlines, original dashed lines, grainlines, notches, and allowances.
  - Cutting layouts at 1:50 scale map pieces onto 150 cm folded satin, 150 cm open piqué, and 300 cm open tulle.
- Technical production plans:
  - Swatches 8.1 to 8.5 measure 50.01 x 50.01 mm, mounted flat with fibre content and fabric finish.
  - Itemised costing sums to exactly $229.15, matching the stated total.
  - Step-by-step order of construction is divided into Dress (8 steps), Overskirt (7 steps), and Collar (5 steps), citing experimental test results. Timeline lists monthly milestones from February to July.
- Product labels: Three sewn labels (Dress, Overskirt, Collar) contain brand name, size, fibre content, country of origin, care text, and the five mandatory ACCC care symbols in proper sequence.

### 4. Investigation, Experimentation and Evaluation (Pages 9 to 12) - Score: 10 / 10

- Breadth and depth of experimentation: Nine experiments across three designated areas (Materials, Equipment, Manufacturing Processes), each with AIM, METHOD, RESULTS, CONCLUSION, quantitative results tables, sample photographs, and identified controls:
  - Experiment 1 (Materials): Tulle weight selection testing stand-off (0, 4, 9 cm), settle time, and skin contact. Mid-weight nylon selected.
  - Experiment 2 (Materials): Collar fabric washing trials over 3 cycles. Cotton piqué selected for opacity and crisp edge retention.
  - Experiment 3 (Materials): Fabric blackness at 3 m and 10 m under hall lights, plus curved seam pressing. Matte crepe-satin face selected.
  - Experiment 4 (Equipment): Machine presser foot feed comparison on tulle. Walking foot selected (1 mm drift vs 11 mm standard foot).
  - Experiment 5 (Equipment): Tier gathering technique. Cord gathering selected (held intervals within 3 mm of 8 cm target).
  - Experiment 6 (Equipment): Fabric marking methods on satin and tulle. Chalk selected for satin, thread tacks for tulle.
  - Experiment 7 (Processes): Tulle edge finishes and satin hem support over 48-hour hanging. Raw tulle edge and 25 mm horsehair braid inside hem selected.
  - Experiment 8 (Processes): Overskirt band closures and quick-change timings across 20 cycles and 3 wearer trials. Three hooks and bars plus two overlap snaps selected (median change time: 48 seconds).
  - Experiment 9 (Processes): Back placket closure under 160 g crosswise load and stage light. Invisible zip and neck hook selected.
- Modification of design and construction: Every experiment explicitly states how findings modified the design or construction, citing the page and step where the result is implemented.
- Evaluation of fibre, yarn, and fabric: Page 12 provides a thorough critical evaluation of polyester continuous-filament crepe satin, nylon monofilament tulle net, and cotton staple piqué in relation to costume performance. Recognises limitations honestly (heat retention, low absorbency, snagging risks, and short-term trial bounds). Component performance table synthesises fibre, performance, and care. Final checks table verifies constructed components against performance criteria.
