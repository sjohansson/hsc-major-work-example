## Section assessment

### Investigation, Experimentation and Evaluation: 9/10

Nine experiments cover materials, equipment, and manufacturing processes. Most methods specify samples, controls, conditions, and measured outcomes. The conclusions point to choices used in the costume: tulle weight, collar fabric, satin face, walking foot, cord gathering, marking tools, hem finish, band closure, and zip. Page 12 evaluates fibre, yarn, and fabric performance against stage visibility, movement, care, heat, and snagging, and it limits conclusions where the trials do not establish long-term performance.

The first top-range dot point is strong in the written methods and decisions, but the plate evidence is not equally secure. Experiment 2 says three 12 cm collar sections were photographed on black satin under one lamp before and after washing; its comparison plates do not make a matched set of those sections and conditions clear. Experiment 4 tests tulle-to-tulle seams, but the caught-area plate does not clearly establish that the depicted seam is the described tulle-to-tulle sample. This makes the evidence for the results less convincing, although the experiments and resulting design choices are otherwise substantial. Under the local placing rule, this places the section at the bottom of the 9 to 10 range: 9 marks.

The other two top-range points are met. Materials, equipment, process, and results are detailed enough to justify choices on comprehensive investigations. The evaluation relates fibre, yarn, and fabric performance to the costume's end use and states the limits of the trials.

### Design Inspiration: 5/5

Pages 1 and 2 explain costume as the focus area and why the source design must read at stage distance and support a quick change. They justify the convertible overskirt and collar, and connect the silhouette, tonal palette, period studies, jet beading, and horsehair braid to specific design decisions. The numbered source studies and captions support the written analysis. The small date discrepancy between the image 2 caption and the cited museum record does not undermine the section's communication or analysis.

### Visual Design Development: 5/5

Pages 3 to 5 show a sequential progression from source-led designs through critique to a resolved final concept. The sketches have front, back, and detail views with numbered features. The modifications and plus/minus/interesting analyses use design elements and principles while identifying functional and aesthetic strengths and weaknesses. The final design photographs are labelled as views of the made design, not misrepresented as sketches. Digital rendering is allowed and is not a fault.

### Manufacturing Specification: 4/5

The description identifies the commercial pattern, drafted pieces, modifications, materials, construction, and finished measures. Production drawings show front and back views, details, pattern pieces, grain/fold information, notches, and layouts. The production plan includes labelled material swatches, quantities, notions, itemised cost, total cost, and construction order. Page 7 labels contain the required fields.

The drawings are not consistently to their stated scale. Drawing 6.1 shows 112 cm shoulder-to-hem at approximately 112 mm, which is correct at 1:10. Its front hem, however, is approximately 60 mm wide. A 240 cm circumference has a 120 cm half-width, or 120 mm at 1:10. Drawing 6.2 repeats the under-width hem. This is a measurable proportion error in the principal production views, so the professional-standard drawing point is not fully met. The remaining top-range points are met, placing the section at 4.
